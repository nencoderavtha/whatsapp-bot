§1

Taking the delivery location from the customer

The bot already has two native WhatsApp mechanisms for this — sendInteractiveLocationRequest (a GPS pin drop) and sendInteractiveAddress (a structured India address form). Uber Direct's dropoff needs both: coordinates and a human-readable line a courier can actually act on.

  1. 01
    Ask for a location pin first. A dropped pin gives exact lat/long — the only thing Uber's routing engine trusts. Text addresses need geocoding and are frequently ambiguous in India (no consistent house numbering, informal lanes).
  2. 02
    Always follow up asking for floor / landmark / gate number. A pin lands on a building's rooftop centroid, not a specific unit. This single follow-up question prevents most "courier couldn't find you" failures.
    e.g. "Got your location 📍 — any flat/floor number, gate code, or landmark to guide the rider?"
  3. 03
    Fall back to the address form if location sharing is declined. sendInteractiveAddress collects name, PIN code, city, street — enough to geocode, though less precise than a pin.
  4. 04
    Validate serviceability immediately by firing the quote call (§2) as soon as a dropoff exists — this is the actual coverage check, not a separate rule you maintain.
Why not just always use the address form?

Because Uber Direct will geocode a text address on its own, and in dense Indian localities that geocoding is often wrong by a full block — enough to strand a courier at the wrong gate. A pin removes that ambiguity; the address form is the fallback, not the default.

§2

Quoting the delivery price

Uber Direct is a two-call flow: request a quote (pickup + dropoff → fee, ETA, and a quote_id), then — only once payment is confirmed — spend that quote to actually create the delivery. Quotes are short-lived, typically expiring in well under an hour, so the timing of these two calls matters more than the calls themselves.

Order → quote → pay → dispatch 
sequenceDiagram
    participant C as Customer
    participant B as Bot
    participant U as Uber Direct
    participant P as Razorpay/UPI
    C->>B: Confirms cart
    B->>C: Requests delivery pin
    C->>B: Shares location + landmark
    B->>U: Create quote (pickup, dropoff)
    U-->>B: fee, eta, quote_id
    B->>C: Shows food + delivery = one total
    C->>P: Pays combined total
    P-->>B: Payment confirmed
    B->>U: Create delivery (quote_id)
    U-->>B: delivery_id, tracking_url
    B->>C: "Courier assigned" + tracking link

The combined total shown to the customer at step 6 must be the number they're actually charged — if the quote has expired by the time they pay, silently re-quoting and charging a different number is the fastest way to lose trust. Re-quote before showing the total if any time has passed, not after.

Design choice

Decide once, in botConfig, whether the raw Uber fee is passed through as-is or adjusted with a markup/subsidy multiplier — and if adjusted, whether that's disclosed as "delivery fee" or split into "delivery" + "service fee" line items on the receipt.

§3

Does the customer pay twice?

No —

if it's wired correctly. Uber Direct is a business-facing logistics API, not a consumer checkout. Uber never talks to your customer's wallet — it bills your restaurant's Uber Direct account for the courier, separately and invisibly, the same way a payment processor bills a merchant. The customer pays one combined amount to the restaurant (food + delivery fee) through the existing Razorpay/UPI flow already in the bot.

The risk isn't a literal double-charge — it's a collection mismatch: if the customer's single payment doesn't actually cover the delivery-fee portion (see Cash on Delivery below), the restaurant ends up paying Uber out of pocket for a fee it never collected.

The cash-on-delivery trap

COD orders are the one case where the "single payment" model breaks. The customer hands cash to the courier (or restaurant) for the food — but Uber still invoices the restaurant's account for the delivery, regardless of how the food was paid for. Over enough orders, that's a real, silent cost leak.

Recommendation

Disable cash-on-delivery for orders with type: "delivery", or require the delivery-fee portion to be paid online even when the food portion is COD. Pickup orders can stay cash-friendly as they are today.

§4

Rider location: live pin, or progress updates?

Worth naming the platform limit up front: WhatsApp's Business/Cloud API can only send a one-time, static location message. The continuously-updating "live location" bubble is a feature of the personal WhatsApp client sharing its own phone's location — a business account cannot push a moving pin into a chat thread, no matter which delivery provider is behind it.

Uber Direct already solves this the way every delivery platform does: each delivery gets a hosted tracking_url — Uber's own live map, updating in real time, no polling required on your side. The right pattern is:

  • Send the tracking_url once, right after dispatch, as a plain-text link (opens in WhatsApp's in-app browser — see the earlier CTA-button fix).
  • Drive discrete WhatsApp status messages off Uber's delivery webhooks — courier assigned, picked up, arriving, delivered — rather than trying to simulate live movement in the chat.
  • Keep an on-demand path: if the customer types "where's my order," poll GET /deliveries/{id} rather than waiting on the next webhook, in case one was missed.
In short

"Live location in the chat" isn't achievable through the Business API — "a live map one tap away, plus milestone updates in the chat" is, and is what customers actually get from Swiggy/Zomato-style tracking today.

§6

Verify before building

Open item

Uber Direct's market coverage and account-onboarding process for India aren't something I can confirm confidently from here — it launched U.S./Canada-first and its footprint has kept expanding, but I don't have reliable current information on availability in your restaurants' cities. Before scoping the build: confirm API access, city-level coverage, and indicative per-delivery pricing directly with Uber (or your Uber Direct account contact) for Hyderabad and wherever else this needs to run.

§7

How this would sit in the existing codebase

Once §6 is confirmed, the shape of the work follows patterns already in the repo:

Service

src/services/uberDirect.ts — OAuth2 client-credentials auth, getQuote(), createDelivery(), getDeliveryStatus(). Same shape as razorpay.ts.

Config

New botConfig fields: uberDirectEnabled, uberDirectCustomerId, pickup lat/long, plus a fee-adjustment setting.

Webhook

New /webhook/uberdirect route alongside the existing /webhook and /webhook/razorpay, signature-verified, feeding status updates back through session-manager.ts.

Order model

Split the stored total into food + delivery-fee fields so refunds and receipts can itemize instead of treating it as one number.
